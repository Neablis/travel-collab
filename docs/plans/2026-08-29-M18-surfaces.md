  reviewed step (`AGENTS.md:161`) and this plan is not it.

## PR 2 — Calendar: the transit split and `N to book`

Both rules live in one pure function, which is what makes them cheap to test.
`calendarCityCards.ts:34-48` already anticipates this work in its own words:
*"When M18 lands, the transit rule refines this function and the cards do not
change shape."* That remains true — `CalendarLens` already renders every
non-arriving group as a one-line strip with a timestamp, so the presentation
exists and only the grouping changes.

### The split rule, precisely

SPEC §12: *"Travel days split at the LAST transit stop. Stops up to and
including it belong to the city you leave; everything after to the city you
arrive in."*

Three consequences the implementation has to get right, none of which the SPEC
spells out:

1. **A day with a transit stop groups binarily** — at most two groups — rather
   than by consecutive city. That is the point: it is what keeps cell heights
   even instead of doubling on travel days. A Tokyo→Nagoya→Kyoto day collapses
   to Tokyo-side and Kyoto-side, and Nagoya is deliberately not a third card.
2. **The departing group's city cannot come from the transit stop itself.** The
   fixture files a travel stop under the city it travels *to*
   (`calendarCityCards.ts:43-46` says so), so the shinkansen's own
   `location.city` is Kyoto while it sits in the Tokyo group. Take the
   departing city from the last **non-transit** city-bearing stop in that group.
3. **If the departing group has no city-bearing non-transit stop, do not
   split.** Fall back to the existing consecutive-city grouping for that day.
   Precedent is in the file: a group that cannot be named renders "an empty
   label and a bare timestamp hanging above the card, which says nothing to
   anyone" — the exact bug fixed after Mitchell walked the #71 preview. A split
   we cannot label is worse than no split.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests in `calendarCityCards.test.ts`**

Cover, at minimum: a day with one transit stop splits into two groups with the
departing city taken from a non-transit stop; a day with two transit stops
splits at the **last**; a day with no transit stop keeps today's
consecutive-city grouping unchanged; a departing group with no nameable city
does not split; `toBook` counts stops whose kind is neither `booked` nor
`transit`; `toBook` is 0 (not null) when everything is booked or transit.

- [ ] **Step 2: Run them and verify they fail** — `pnpm --filter web test calendarCityCards`

- [ ] **Step 3: Add `toBook` and `departsAt` to `CityCard`**

```ts
/** Stops here whose kind is neither `booked` nor `transit`. 0 when none. */
toBook: number;
/**
 * The departure — the start of the transit stop that closed this group.
 * Null when this group was not closed by a transit stop, in which case a
 * strip falls back to `firstStart`.
 */
departsAt: string | null;
```

`firstStart` keeps its current meaning (the group's earliest start). It is not
the departure: a Tokyo morning with breakfast at 7:00 and a shinkansen at 8:20
has `firstStart: "07:00"` and `departsAt: "08:20"`, and SPEC §12 is explicit
that the strip shows the transit stop's start.

- [ ] **Step 4: Implement the split**, ahead of the existing consecutive-city
      path, with the no-nameable-city fallback from consequence 3.

- [ ] **Step 5: Compute `toBook` per group** in the existing `groups.map`.

- [ ] **Step 6: Render `N to book` in `CalendarLens`** — warning tint, only
      when `> 0`, on the arriving card. Use `departsAt ?? firstStart` for the
      strip's timestamp.

- [ ] **Step 7: Run `pnpm --filter web test calendarCityCards CalendarLens`, then root `pnpm lint` and `pnpm typecheck`**

- [ ] **Step 8: Commit and open PR 2**
