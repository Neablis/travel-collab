### KI-2026-09-06-g — the Map lens can be scrolled far past its end on mobile, and `100dvh` is the likely engine

- **Severity:** correctness (user-visible), reproduced by report twice, never in a harness. Intermittent by nature.
- **Area:** `apps/web/src/components/lenses/MapLens.tsx:90-104` (the `canvasTop` measurement) and `:526-529` (the canvas height).
- **The reports, six days apart, and the second answers the first.**
  - 2026-08-30 design pass, finding 12: *"I was able to scroll way past the bottom, not sure how, its not a always thing"* — left open, unreproduced, with an explicit **"Needs: which lens, and what you had just done"**. The guess recorded there was a drag preview appended to `<body>` by pragmatic-drag-and-drop.
  - 2026-09-06 preview: *"on the map view page on mobile, I'm able to scroll way off the page"* — `/trips/…?lens=Map`, Chrome 152 on Android, 912×1685. **That names the lens.** The drag guess is not ruled out but no longer needed: the Map lens is the one surface here sized from the viewport rather than from its content.
- **Mechanism, as a hypothesis with a mechanism rather than a hunch.** The canvas is sized `calc(100dvh - canvasTop - var(--rack-height) - var(--phone-tab-bar-height))`. `dvh` is the **dynamic** viewport unit: on Android Chrome it grows as the URL bar collapses. So a page with any initial overflow admits a loop — scroll a little → the browser chrome collapses → `dvh` grows → the canvas, sized in `dvh`, grows → the document grows → more scroll is available → repeat. Each turn adds roughly the toolbar's height. That matches both reports: it needs *some* initial overflow to start, which is exactly why it is "not an always thing".
- **Why `dvh` is there, and why the obvious swap is not free.** The comment at the height says `dvh` was chosen over `vh` because "on mobile the two differ by the browser chrome's height, and `vh` is the one that overflows" — `100vh` is the *large* viewport and overflows whenever the chrome is showing. **`svh` is the third option and the one that fits**: the *small* viewport is the largest height that cannot overflow at any chrome state, so it closes the loop by construction. The cost is the symptom that finding 8 of the same 2026-08-30 pass fixed — "a strip of page visible under the map on a tall window" — which would come back by exactly the toolbar's height while the toolbar is collapsed.
- **Not verified, and it cannot be here.** The loop needs a browser with a dynamic toolbar. Playwright's desktop Chromium has none, so `dvh`, `svh` and `lvh` are all the same number in this harness and no test written here can tell the three apart. This is the same class of limit as `KI-2026-09-06-e`.
- **The cheapest confirmation, if a device is to hand:** on the Map lens, scroll down once and watch whether the scrollable distance *grows* with each swipe rather than ending. Growth confirms the loop; a fixed overshoot that stops points elsewhere — most likely back at the drag-preview guess from 2026-08-30.
- **A detail that narrows it further:** the report is at **912px**, which is above the 768px breakpoint, so `--phone-tab-bar-height` is `0px` from the media query AND the bar is `md:hidden` so its published measurement is zero too. Both terms of that subtraction are genuinely zero at this width — which rules the tab-bar reservation out as the cause and leaves the viewport unit.
